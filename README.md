# Nepal Flood Impact & Disaster Analysis | Power BI Dashboard

An interactive Power BI dashboard created to analyze flood incidents, human impact, infrastructure damage, economic loss, and flood severity across Nepal from 2015 to 2025.

> **Important:** This dashboard uses a synthetic dataset created for data analytics practice. Figures do not represent official Government of Nepal or NDRRMA statistics.

---

## 📊 Project Overview

Floods can cause serious damage to people, homes, infrastructure, agriculture, and the economy.

This project uses Power BI to explore flood-related data and identify patterns across:

- Flood incidents
- People affected
- People displaced
- Deaths and injuries
- Houses and bridges damaged
- Roads damaged
- Agricultural land affected
- Economic loss
- Flood severity
- Rainfall
- River water levels
- Regional and yearly trends

The goal of this project is to demonstrate practical **data analysis, data visualization, and dashboard development skills**.

---

## 🎯 Project Objectives

- Analyze flood incidents over time
- Identify the most affected provinces and districts
- Understand the relationship between flood severity and human impact
- Analyze infrastructure damage
- Compare economic losses across provinces
- Study rainfall and river water level patterns
- Identify high-impact flood severity levels
- Build an interactive dashboard for easier decision-making

## 💡 Key Insights

Based on the synthetic dataset:

- Severe flood incidents have the highest human impact.
- Severe floods account for the highest number of people affected and displaced.
- Severe flood events also show the highest economic loss.
- Madhesh has the highest total people affected among the provinces in the dataset.
- Some districts consistently appear among the most affected areas.
- Higher rainfall is associated with higher people affected in the synthetic data.
- River water level increases across higher flood severity categories.
- Infrastructure damage increases significantly with flood severity.

> These observations are based only on the synthetic dataset and should not be interpreted as real-world Nepal flood statistics.
